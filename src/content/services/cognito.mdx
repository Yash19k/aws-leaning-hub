Cognito handles **accounts for your app's users**: sign-up, sign-in, password resets, email or phone verification, multi-factor authentication (MFA), and "Sign in with Google" style logins. After a user signs in, Cognito hands your app signed **tokens** (JWTs) that prove who they are. Your backend checks the token instead of storing and checking passwords itself.

Don't confuse it with [IAM](/services/iam). IAM controls who can manage your AWS account and resources. Cognito is for the people using the app you built.

## Key ideas

<KeyConcepts>
  <Concept title="User pool">A user directory with sign-up and sign-in. It stores users, enforces password and MFA rules, sends verification codes, and issues tokens.</Concept>
  <Concept title="Tokens">An **ID token** says who the user is (email, name, groups), an **access token** says what they may call, and a **refresh token** gets new ones when they expire, so users stay signed in.</Concept>
  <Concept title="App client and managed login">An **app client** is one app (web, mobile) that may use the pool. **Managed login** (formerly the Hosted UI) is a ready-made sign-in page on a Cognito domain, so you don't have to build forms TODO(verify).</Concept>
  <Concept title="Identity pool">Swaps a sign-in (from a user pool or a social provider) for **temporary AWS credentials** with an IAM role, so the app can call AWS services directly.</Concept>
  <Concept title="Lambda triggers">Hooks where your own Lambda functions run during sign-up or sign-in, e.g. to only allow your college's email domain, or to add custom claims to tokens.</Concept>
  <Concept title="Groups">Put users into groups such as `admin`, and the group names appear in their tokens for your backend to check.</Concept>
</KeyConcepts>

## Where it would fit in our projects

Both projects build accounts themselves, which makes them good to learn from, and Cognito is the managed alternative:

- **[DropVault](/projects/dropvault)** has an `auth-service` Lambda (PBKDF2 password hashes, HMAC-signed tokens, a DynamoDB users table) and a **Lambda authorizer** that checks the token before every file route. DropVault started as a fork of an app whose auth service was built on Cognito; its sign-up was later simplified and moved to the built-in service.
- **[Quibble](/projects/quibble)** stores bcrypt password hashes in its users table and manages its own sessions.

With Cognito, the user pool would replace the auth code and users table, and API Gateway's Cognito (REST) or JWT (HTTP API) authorizer could replace the custom token check.

## Free Tier and cost

<Callout type="cost" title="How Cognito is billed">

User pools are billed per **monthly active user** (MAU): a user who signs in, signs up or refreshes a token in that month. Since late 2024 there are feature tiers (Lite, Essentials, Plus) with different prices TODO(verify).

- A free allowance of monthly active users applies to the lower tiers TODO(verify), which covers a class project many times over.
- SMS for verification codes and MFA is billed separately, through SNS TODO(verify). Email codes or authenticator apps avoid that.

</Callout>
