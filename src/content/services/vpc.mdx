A VPC is **your own private network inside AWS**. You choose its range of private IP addresses, split it into **subnets**, and decide with **route tables** and firewalls which parts can talk to the internet and which can't. Servers, databases and caches ([EC2](/services/ec2), [RDS](/services/rds), [ElastiCache](/services/elasticache)) always live inside a VPC.

Every region in your account comes with a **default VPC**, already set up so that new instances get a public IP. It's handy for experiments; real projects usually build their own.

## Key ideas

<KeyConcepts>
  <Concept title="CIDR block">The VPC's address range, written like `10.0.0.0/16` (about 65,000 addresses). Each subnet takes a slice, e.g. `10.0.1.0/24`.</Concept>
  <Concept title="Public and private subnets">A subnet lives in one Availability Zone. It's **public** when its route table sends internet traffic (`0.0.0.0/0`) to an **internet gateway**. A **private** subnet has no such route, so nothing on the internet can reach it directly.</Concept>
  <Concept title="NAT gateway">Lets resources in a private subnet make outgoing internet calls (like downloading updates) without being reachable from outside. It's billed per hour and per GB TODO(verify).</Concept>
  <Concept title="Security groups">Firewalls on each resource (instance, database, Lambda network interface). **Stateful**: if a request is allowed in, its reply is allowed out. Allow rules only.</Concept>
  <Concept title="Network ACLs">Optional firewalls on a whole subnet. **Stateless**: you must allow both directions. Can deny as well as allow.</Concept>
  <Concept title="VPC endpoints">Private paths from your VPC to AWS services. **Gateway endpoints** for S3 and DynamoDB are free and work through route tables; **interface endpoints** for other services cost per hour TODO(verify).</Concept>
</KeyConcepts>

## Where it would fit in our projects

Neither project creates a VPC, and neither runs its Lambda functions in one. Every service they use ([API Gateway](/services/api-gateway), [DynamoDB](/services/dynamodb), [S3](/services/s3), [SES](/services/ses), [SNS](/services/sns)) is reached over AWS's public HTTPS endpoints, protected by [IAM](/services/iam). That's simpler and avoids NAT gateway costs.

A VPC would appear the moment you add something private. If [Quibble](/projects/quibble) moved to an RDS database, its Lambda functions would join private subnets with a security group that the database trusts, and DynamoDB access could go through a free gateway endpoint. Quibble's question bank covers most of this page: public subnets, NAT gateways, security groups and gateway endpoints.

<Callout type="cost" title="How VPC is billed">

VPCs, subnets, route tables, internet gateways, security groups and gateway endpoints cost nothing TODO(verify). The things to watch are:

- **NAT gateways**: per hour plus per GB processed, even with no traffic TODO(verify). Delete them after practice.
- **Interface endpoints**: per hour per Availability Zone, plus per GB TODO(verify).
- **Public IPv4 addresses**: charged per hour TODO(verify).
