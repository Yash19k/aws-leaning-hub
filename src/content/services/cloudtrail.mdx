CloudTrail records **who did what in your AWS account**. Almost every action, whether made from the console, the CLI, an SDK or another AWS service, is an API call, and CloudTrail logs each one as an **event**: who made it, from which IP address, when, what they asked for, and whether it worked.

It answers questions like "who deleted that bucket?", "which access key created these EC2 instances in a region we never use?" and "what did my deploy actually change?".

[CloudWatch](/services/cloudwatch) and CloudTrail are easy to mix up. CloudWatch watches how your **app** is doing (logs, metrics, alarms). CloudTrail watches what is done **to your account**.

## Key ideas

<KeyConcepts>
  <Concept title="Event history">On by default in every account: the last 90 days of **management events**, searchable in the console, at no charge TODO(verify).</Concept>
  <Concept title="Management vs data events">**Management events** change resources (`CreateBucket`, `RunInstances`, `CreateStack`). **Data events** are actions on the data itself (an S3 `GetObject`, a Lambda `Invoke`). Data events are not logged unless you turn them on, because there are so many of them.</Concept>
  <Concept title="Trail">A trail copies events to an [S3](/services/s3) bucket (and optionally CloudWatch Logs) so you can keep them as long as you like. An **organization trail** covers every account in an AWS Organization.</Concept>
  <Concept title="Event fields">Each event is JSON with `eventName`, `eventSource`, `userIdentity` (the user, role or service), `sourceIPAddress`, `requestParameters` and `errorCode` if it failed.</Concept>
  <Concept title="CloudTrail Lake">A managed store where you query events with SQL, as an alternative to trails in S3 TODO(verify).</Concept>
</KeyConcepts>

## Where it would fit in our projects

Neither project sets up CloudTrail, but it's already recording their deploys. When you run `sam deploy` for [Quibble](/projects/quibble), event history shows CloudFormation calls such as `CreateChangeSet` and `ExecuteChangeSet`, followed by the `CreateFunction`, `CreateTable` and other calls CloudFormation made for you. For [DropVault](/projects/dropvault), each `terraform apply` shows up as the individual API calls Terraform made with your credentials.

Quibble's admin audit log, which it keeps in DynamoDB, is the same idea at the app level: a record of who banned which player and when.

## Try it

See the five most recent events in your current region:

<Command code={`aws cloudtrail lookup-events --max-results 5 --query "Events[].[EventTime,EventName,Username]" --output table`} />

Find who called a specific API, such as deleting an S3 bucket:

<Command code={`aws cloudtrail lookup-events --lookup-attributes AttributeKey=EventName,AttributeValue=DeleteBucket --max-results 5`} />

## Free Tier and cost

<Callout type="cost" title="How CloudTrail is billed">

- **Event history** (90 days of management events) is free TODO(verify).
- The **first trail** that delivers management events is free; extra copies of management events cost per 100,000 events TODO(verify).
- **Data events** and **CloudTrail Lake** are charged per event or per GB TODO(verify).
- You also pay normal S3 storage for the log files a trail writes.

</Callout>
